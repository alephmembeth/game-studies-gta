\section{Other Occurrences}\label{sec:app_other_occurrences}

In addition to the four major clusters discussed in the main text (aliens and UFOs, mind control, the covert sphere, and surveillance), there are countless smaller instances of conspiracy narratives, some of which -- for the sake of completeness -- we want to briefly describe below.

\paragraph{Anti-vaccination.}
Resistance to vaccination is nearly as old as the practice itself, but its present form dates back to 1998, when the British gastroenterologist Andrew Wakefield published a paper in \textit{The Lancet} claiming a link between the measles, mumps and rubella vaccine and autism.
The paper was later retracted and its author struck off the medical register in 2010; the false claim and its repercussions have outlived both.
In \textit{GTA~V}, on Blaine County Talk Radio, the host Bobby June runs the talk show \enquote{Bless Your Heart}.
During a conversation with conservative politician and stuntman Jock Cranley, she goes on a tirade against immunisations:

\begin{quote}
   \enquote{Listen, Jock, before we get started, now, you mentioned it earlier:
   I'm really fired up about vaccines and immunization in this country, and I mean, these shots will give you a disease.
   And wait for your body to just fight it out.
   It's horrible.
   Listen, we never got my stepson Randy shots, and he was just happy as a peach, living his life to the fullest right up to the end when he died naturally of polio at the age of seven.}
\end{quote}

\noindent Ronald Jakowski, whose call-in show can be heard on Blaine County Talk Radio, has a caller stating:
\enquote{I mean, thanks to big pharma, all my children are crippled from vaccines.}
The caller's wife then screams from the background:
\enquote{They're crippled 'cause you drink and drive!}

\paragraph{Big Pharma.}
Patents and profit motives can be made to explain almost anything.
In regard to the pharmaceutical industry, the claim is characteristically that drug companies withhold cures they already possess or that they manufacture the illnesses they subsequently treat.
In \textit{GTA~IV}, the talk station Public Liberty Radio leans into this suspicion -- the issue is touched upon during an episode of the health show \enquote{Pacemaker}.

\paragraph{Chemtrails.}
The narrative that the trails behind high-flying aircraft are not condensed water vapour but rather a deliberate aerial spraying emerged in the late 1990s and reached a mass audience from 1999 through the late-night talk radio of Arthur Bell.
In the \textit{GTA~IV} expansion \textit{The Ballad of Gay Tony}, We Know The Truth Talk Radio features the show \enquote{Conspire}.
At one point, the show's host, John Smith, asks a caller:
\enquote{What's on your mind?}
To which the caller says:
\enquote{Contrails, John.
How do we know, when we see a plane flying over, and leaving these clouds behind them, that it isn't biological or chemical agents being sprayed?
I mean, if you live in a poor area, they fly over and leave these vapor trails, full of government chemicals to make you pregnant.}
Contrails are also discussed by Ronald Jakowski on his show on Blaine County Talk Radio.

\paragraph{Election denial.}
Disputed American elections long predate the present century.
For example, the outcome of the 1876 presidential election was settled by a specially convened electoral commission.
In \textit{GTA Liberty City Stories}, media mogul Donald Love runs for mayor of Liberty City against Miles O'Donovan, and the player acts as part of his campaign apparatus in a string of missions.
In \enquote{Steering The Vote}, the player character, Toni Cipriani, drives a bandwagon through the city broadcasting smears (e.\,g. \enquote{O'Donovan's a communist!}); in \enquote{Cam-Pain}, he kills the opposition's campaign workers; and in \enquote{Friggin' The Riggin'}, he torches pallets of forged ballots -- since both parties are playing unfairly.
Finally, in \enquote{Counterfeit Count}, with the exit polls \enquote{neck and neck}, Toni intercepts a ballot van so that Love's people can \enquote{steal some of the uncounted votes, and replace them with some of our own} -- whereupon Love, who had earlier assured Toni that \enquote{I'm no Democrat, but even I draw the line at vote rigging}, is reminded of the fact and snaps: \enquote{Oh, shut up!}
In \textit{GTA~V}, marijuana legalisation advocate Barry accosts passersby with the claim that a referendum has been stolen; a statement he also repeats in his conversation with Michael:
\enquote{They rigged the vote, I saw the ballots, it's a lie.}

\paragraph{Elvis lives.}
Real-world Elvis Presley died on 16~August 1977 in Memphis, Tennessee -- or so we are told.
To this day, there are claims that he did not actually die on that day.
In \textit{GTA~2}, there is a reference to Elvis in the form of a group of Elvis impersonators who walk through the streets in a long line.
If the player runs all of them over, the real-world catchphrase \enquote{Elvis Has Left The Building} appears on the screen (and is read aloud enthusiastically).
If, instead, the player comes across them on foot, they might hear one of them say: \enquote{I told you he was alive.}
In \textit{GTA~III}, then, newspapers -- issues of the \textit{Liberty Cock} -- are blown across the ground, with headlines reading \enquote{Zombie Elvis Found}.
Players can see it also displayed as an aerial banner trailing behind an aeroplane.\footnote{Note that the Definitive Edition compilation changes the headline in \textit{GTA~III} and \textit{GTA Vice City} to \enquote{Zombie Rocker Found}.}
This reappears in the PC version of \textit{GTA Vice City}.
Moreover, in \textit{GTA~V}, players can find a mural depicting Frank Sinatra, Marilyn Monroe, Elvis Presley, and Charlie Chaplin (based on a real mural from Hollywood); as the series has evolved, it has gradually moved away from direct references to the real world (real names of politicians, organisations and so forth are less and less common), but Elvis shows up again here nonetheless.

\paragraph{Fake global warming.}
Claims that anthropogenic climate change is a fabrication have been a recurring position in American politics.
The show \enquote{Conspire} on \textit{The Ballad of Gay Tony}'s We Know The Truth Talk Radio has a caller stating:
\enquote{Yeah, I wanna talk about global warming.
Global warming is a real estate hoax to drive up the prices of mountain properties. [\dots]
It's a myth -- I mean, it's a \textit{lie}.
Where I live, the weather is always the same.
I mean, every day.
It's not getting warmer.
Last week was pretty cold.}
The topic returns in \textit{GTA~V}, where Ronald Jakowski has Sophia Bowles-Carmichael as a guest on his show on Blaine County Talk Radio, where she, i.\,a., rambles:
\enquote{People talk about so-called sea level rising.
Sea level rising!
It is a fact that thousands of years ago, the sea rose and flooded everything, but a bunch of animals were on a cruise ship and they were saved.
That's a truth that isn't taught at schools!}

\paragraph{Fake history.}
The suspicion that certain historical events are not true is a recurring theme.
In addition to the above-mentioned Elvis and the below-mentioned moon landing, examples include the following instances: In the very first \textit{GTA}, the player helps a character named Mason to escape from a military base with top-secret files.
Mason remarks:
\enquote{I got the files.
Shit!
Aliens.
Kennedys.
Gremlin.
Everythin'!
Let's get outta here!}
In \textit{GTA San Andreas}, during the mission \enquote{Wear Flowers in your Hair}, The Truth tells CJ:
\enquote{Now, what if I told you we never went to the moon, JFK lives in Scotland with Janis Joplin, and the only reason we've been in a cold war for the last forty-five years was because snake-headed aliens run the oil business?}
In the mission \enquote{Verdant Meadows}, Mike Toreno reads a book titled \textit{Conspiracy Theories}, before mentioning to CJ:
\enquote{It's amazing. [\dots] This history.
It's all lies.
It says Hitler killed himself and that we nuked Japan.
And people believe this shit.
Jesus.
Well, if it makes them sleep better at night, I guess.}
In \textit{The Ballad of Gay Tony}'s We Know The Truth Talk Radio's show \enquote{Conspire}, a guest promotes her new book saying:
\enquote{Kids can color in swastikas while they learn about how Pearl Harbour was an inside job.}
In \textit{GTA San Andreas}, the player can hear about the Epsilon Program's beliefs on West Coast Talk Radio, while in \textit{GTA~V}, they can read about it on the Epsilonists' in-game website, epsilonprogram.com.
The Epsilonists teach, i.\,a., that the Earth is only 157 years old and that dinosaurs are a lie.
Speaking of the Earth:
The magazine \textit{monomania}, which can be found in \textit{GTA~V}, runs, i.\,a., the headline \enquote{Hollow Earth -- Room to Let}.
And, finally, speaking of dinosaurs:
The in-game television series \textit{Kung Fu Rainbow Lazer Force} on Weazel features a teacher who has written \enquote{DINOSAURS WERE HUNTED AND DOMESTICATED} on his whiteboard, teaching that \enquote{a \textit{giant} flood [\dots] destroyed the dinosaurs and the Romans}.

\paragraph{Fake moon landing.}
The first crewed moon landing took place on 20~July 1969 as part of NASA's Apollo programme.
To this day, images taken by NASA's Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter show the traces left behind at the landing sites.
Nevertheless, some people still claim that the landing was nothing but a hoax.
This idea is echoed in \textit{GTA Vice City}.
Here, the player finds a hangar in a movie studio where a set depicting the moon landing has been built -- complete with a lunar module and an American flag.
In \textit{GTA San Andreas}, there's a caller on West Coast Talk Radio's show \enquote{Area~53}, saying to host Marvin Trill:
\enquote{I agree with you about the moon.
We never landed on the moon.
It's such a good fake.}
Also, in the mission \enquote{Wear Flowers in your Hair}, The Truth tells CJ:
\enquote{Now, what if I told you we never went to the moon, [\dots]?}

\paragraph{Mainstream media.}
The term \enquote*{mainstream media} is used about news organisations rather than by them.
Those who employ it mean the large established outlets, and the claim attached to it is characteristically not that these outlets report badly, but that they report in concert and in someone's interest.
The narrative of biased \enquote*{mainstream media} is taken up, among other places, in \textit{The Ballad of Gay Tony}.
There, the website of the Angels of Death motorcycle club, angelsofdeathonline.com, has an FAQ page that includes the question:
\enquote{What's this we've heard in the news about a war with the Lost MC?}
The answer begins as follows:
\enquote{The news media has been lying to the American People [sic] since the founding fathers signed the Declaration of Independence.
Look at the unfairness with which they treat our president.}
In \textit{GTA~V}, editor Cynthia Kelly reports for \textit{Weazel News}:
\enquote{A Gruppe Sachs [sic] armoured car being used by the IAA to transport bonds was raided yesterday and the mainstream media refuses to call it what it is, a terror attack.}
Finally, in an ironic bout of self-reference, in both \textit{The Lost and Damned} and \textit{The Ballad of Gay Tony}, there is an article on the \textit{Weazel News} website titled \enquote{Lazy days for lackadaisical generation}.
In it, \textit{Weazel} -- the embodiment of the mainstream media machine -- claims \textit{not} to be part of the mainstream:
\enquote{The mainstream media is to blame, which we are not by any means the mainstream media [sic], despite the fact that we have a TV station in every city with at least one stoplight and are on every cable box in America.
We're outsiders, we're angry [\dots].}

\paragraph{New World Order and Illuminati.}
During the Cold War, author Gary Allen played a key role in popularising the term \enquote*{New World Order} through his books (e.\,g. Allen and Abraham, 1971).
While the term was initially focused on a perceived communist menace, it later came to encompass other global power structures as well.
In \textit{GTA Vice City Stories}, this is echoed in the radio show \enquote{New World Order} on Vice City Public Radio.
While the entire show is a parody of the American self-image and its views of the world, one caller stands out in the context of conspiracy narratives.
The caller -- a customs and immigration official -- starts off by saying:
\enquote{I wanted to ask:
What's the deal with the one-dollar bill?}
To which the show's host, Dwayne Thorn, replies:
\enquote{What do you mean?}
The caller goes on:
\enquote{Well, there's an eyeball on top of a pyramid, and it says \enquote{annuit cœptis}.
Hell, if that eyeball don't creep me out enough \dots}
Thorn interrupts him, clarifying:
\enquote{It's Latin, I think.}
To which the caller responds:
\enquote{Well, they should keep foreign languages off of our money.
I mean, pretty soon, we'll all speak Latin.
And what are you gonna do then?}
In \textit{GTA Chinatown Wars}, Ammu-Nation advertises as follows:
\enquote{Valued customer, the New World Order is coming for your guns, so we're slashing prices. Take a look at the following!}

Closely related is the Order of the Illuminati.
It was founded at Ingolstadt in 1776 by Adam Weishaupt, a professor of canon law, and banned by the Bavarian government in 1785.
Its posthumous career has been considerably longer and more successful than its actual one.
Here and there, \textit{GTA} games contain scattered references to the Illuminati.
In \textit{GTA Vice City Stories}, for example, the radio show \enquote{New World Order} on Vice City Public Radio has a logo of a pyramid with an eye at the top (also, the logo of the radio station Self-Actualization FM combines the motifs of a pyramid and an eye).\footnote{For further instances of this symbolism, see \enquote{All Seeing Eye} (n.\,d.).}
Concentric circles rise upward from its tip, and at its base, the words \enquote{\textsc{NOVUS ORDO SECLORUM}} are written (also see Knight, 2002, pp.~1--2).
In \textit{GTA~V}, Ronald Jakowski mentions the Illuminati explicitly on his radio show on Blaine County Talk Radio.

\paragraph{Red Scare.}
The fear of communist infiltration swept through the United States in two great waves -- the first after the Russian Revolution in 1917, the second led by Senator Joseph McCarthy around the 1950s -- and it proved remarkably durable, outliving the accusations and, eventually, the enemy itself.
In the \textit{GTA} series, it is present, amongst many other examples, in the Ammu-Nation radio commercial, which runs in \textit{GTA~III} and \textit{GTA Vice City} and can be heard inside the stores in \textit{GTA San Andreas}, introducing the gun store franchise as \enquote{the store leading the fight against communism}.
Customers waiting out the mandatory ten-minute period are invited to \enquote{fire off a few rounds at the Ammu-Nation gun range, featuring faces of famous commie pinkos}, and the weekend brings an Ammu-Nation Film Festival with \enquote{free screenings of the documentary \textit{Red Dawn}}.
The conspiracy narrative that communists have infiltrated Hollywood is explored in the episode \enquote{Moorehead and the Soviet Tit Wank or Reds Under the Bed} of the animated TV series \textit{Moorehead Rides Again!}, which can be seen on TVs in \textit{GTA~V}.\footnote{In this context, the fear of communism is already a theme in \textit{GTA Liberty City Stories}, where \enquote{Moorehead} is still a radio show.}
The police chief succinctly summarises the essence of the matter to the series' main character, Gordon Moorehead:
\enquote{We think that commies have infiltrated the moving pictures!
They're writing subversive themes into scripts to influence our children and women!}
(The general fear of communism is also reflected in the in-game TV series \textit{Republican Space Rangers}.)

\paragraph{Virus scepticism.}
In June 2009, the World Health Organization raised its influenza pandemic alert to phase 6 over the H1N1 virus.
Within months, the virus had become the object of conspiracy narratives claiming, i.\,a., that it was manufactured.
Similar narratives occurred during the COVID-19 pandemic beginning in early 2020.
The topic is briefly touched upon in \textit{GTA~V} by Duane Earl on his show \enquote{Beyond Insemination} on Blaine County Talk Radio, talking about bird flu and swine flu.
